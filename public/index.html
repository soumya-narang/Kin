A care companion on Alexa+

Mum says she's fine. Kin listens to the rest.

Kamala is 74 and lives alone in Pune. Her children live in other cities. She won't install an app, but she talks to the speaker in her kitchen every day. Kin turns those everyday words into care her family can see.

“I'm fine.”Every parent, on every phone call.

The dizzy spell after her bath, the tablet she forgot twice, the quiet evenings. None of it makes it into a five-minute call, and none of it sounds serious on its own.

Amazon retired Alexa Together, its caregiving service, in 2024. The need didn't go anywhere. Kin brings it back, built for Alexa+.

Her own words, not a status code

How Kin behaves around someone's mother.

Patterns, not pings.

One dizzy spell isn't an alert. Dizziness that keeps lining up with missed blood-pressure doses is. Each pattern alerts the family at most once every 12 hours.

Some things can't wait.

A fall, chest pain or trouble breathing skips the pattern entirely. Kin tells Kamala to call emergency services and tells the family straight away.

It speaks first, gently.

Good morning, Kamala. How did you sleep last night? when nothing has been heard yet that morning, and a nudge when a tablet is half an hour late, long before it counts as missed.

Her words, not a score.

The family sees what Kamala actually said, and every alert explains why it was sent. Nothing is reduced to a red number.

It never diagnoses.

Kin reports what was said and what it noticed, and suggests a call or a word with the doctor. It never tells anyone to change their medication.
